# EmoVoice-DB speech emotion recognition (single-corpus baseline)

Trains the `cls` system (fine-tuned WavLM Large + attentive statistics pooling + FC
head) on EmoVoice-DB alone and scores it on the corpus's own test split,
classifying each utterance into one of seven emotions.

[EmoVoice-DB](https://huggingface.co/datasets/yhaha/EmoVoice-DB) is an English
emotional speech dataset **synthesised entirely with GPT-4o-audio** from texts
and emotion descriptions written by GPT-4o; samples with a high word error rate
were filtered out (dataset card). It is not human speech. The lists name 22,100
recordings, 40.44 hours in total, 1.05 to 27.75 seconds long, all 24 kHz mono
16-bit wav (converted to 16 kHz here). The emotions are `angry`, `disgust`
(`disgusted` in the lists), `fear` (`fearful`), `happy`, `neutral`, `sad` and
`surprise` (`surprised`), roughly balanced (2,950 to 3,486 recordings each). The
card says five voices were used; the voice name is the last part of each key
(`ash`, `ballad`, `coral`, `sage`, `verse`) and every voice appears in every split.

The label is the `emotion` field of the official lists. Each recording also has a
free-text emotion description, which this recipe does not use. The label is the
emotion that was requested from the model ("Repeat this sentence with the emotion of
<description>: <text>", from the paper's dataset description), not an emotion that listeners heard; the card
describes no listening check, only the removal of samples with a high word error rate.

## The audio comes from GPT-4o-audio, a model with undisclosed training data

This is a different matter from the data merely being synthetic, so it is stated
here on its own. Every recording was generated by OpenAI's GPT-4o-audio
(`gpt-4o-audio-preview`, accessed through OpenAI's service; the sentences and the
emotion descriptions were written by GPT-4o). What the sources say (read on
2026-10-24):

- **Training data is not disclosed.** The
  [GPT-4o system card](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21276) says the model's text and
  voice abilities were pre-trained on data up to October 2023 from "select publicly
  available data, mostly collected from industry-standard machine learning datasets
  and web crawls" and "proprietary data from data partnerships", and that the data
  includes audio. It names no dataset or source, and says nothing about the licences of
  the speech used. So the origin and terms of the speech the model learned from are
  unknown.
- **Terms of use.** OpenAI's
  [Services Agreement](https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/) (the version
  effective 2026-01-01, which we read; the version in force when EmoVoice-DB was made
  in early 2025, and which terms apply to this use of the API, were not checked)
  assigns the output to the customer (section 4.1) but does not allow the customer
  to "use Output to develop artificial intelligence models that compete with OpenAI's
  products and services", except for permitted uses such as classifiers that are not
  distributed or made commercially available (sections 3.3(e) and 17). Whether
  training a speech emotion recogniser on this audio falls under that clause is not
  something the dataset's MIT licence or the paper addresses.
- **The dataset's own licence.** The Hugging Face card states MIT. It does not mention
  OpenAI's terms; the card and the paper do not discuss how the two relate.
- **Why it matters for use.** Because the training data of the generating model is
  unknown, and its terms may apply to the output, use of this data needs to be
  considered from that angle as well as the usual one for synthetic speech. This
  readme does not decide whether the data should be used.

## Provenance and licence

The files are downloaded from the Hugging Face dataset `yhaha/EmoVoice-DB` (no
login; not gated), pinned to commit `e0e61055fc5c2b52bc20d8b45f8ce3037228a61d`
and checked against the SHA-256 digests in `dataset/config.yaml`: the three
split lists (`train.jsonl`, `val.jsonl`, `test.jsonl`) and the seven
`audio/<emotion>.zip` archives, 5.8 GB in total. The card states the MIT licence.
The audio was generated with OpenAI's GPT-4o-audio (see the section above for what
is known about its training data and terms). Cite Yang et al., "EmoVoice:
LLM-based Emotional Text-To-Speech Model with Freestyle Text Prompting",
arXiv:2504.12867.

The archives hold 24,923 recordings, 2,823 more than the lists name (for example
414 more `angry` than listed). Only the listed ones are used (the official lists
define the splits, and the extra recordings are not in any split); why the others are
in the archives is not documented. They may be samples removed for a high word error
rate, but the card does not say so.

Two environment variables control where the data lives. Both are optional and
default to `download/` and `data/` under the recipe.

- `EMOVOICEDB` — the directory holding the three lists and the extracted `<emotion>/` folders, or where to download them to
- `EMOVOICEDB_OUTPUT` — where the converted audio and manifests are written

## Download

`prepare_source` downloads the lists and archives, checks them and extracts the
wav files. Fetching the 5.8 GB with `curl` took 11 min 33 s, and extracting the
archives took about 50 s. `create_dataset`, `remove_long_short` and
`prepare_labels` together took 3 min 1 s on one run (8 conversion threads; the
download was already done).

## Splits

The official lists are used as they are. `train.jsonl` has three rows per
recording (one for each of three paraphrased emotion descriptions), 63,150 rows
for 21,050 recordings; rows are de-duplicated by key. No recording is in more than
one split. The five voices are shared by all splits, so scores are not
voice-independent. The test and validation sets have the same count in every class.

| split | utterances | hours | after `remove_long_short` |
|---|---|---|---|
| train | 21,050 | 38.50 | 21,050 |
| valid | 350 | 0.64 | 350 |
| test | 700 | 1.30 | 700 (not filtered) |

## Quick start

Run the three commands separately. With several GPUs, `train` starts one
process per GPU by re-running its own command, so any other stage listed in the
same command would run once per GPU as well.

```bash
python run.py --stages create_dataset remove_long_short prepare_labels \
    collect_stats --training_config conf/training.yaml
python run.py --stages train \
    --training_config conf/training.yaml
python run.py --stages infer measure \
    --training_config conf/training.yaml \
    --inference_config conf/inference.yaml \
    --metrics_config conf/metrics.yaml
```
