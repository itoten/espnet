# EmotionTalk speech emotion recognition (single-corpus baseline)

Trains the `cls` system (fine-tuned WavLM Large + attentive statistics pooling + FC
head) on EmotionTalk alone and scores it on EmotionTalk's own test split, classifying
each utterance into one of seven emotions.

[EmotionTalk](https://huggingface.co/datasets/BAAI/Emotiontalk) (Sun et al., "EmotionTalk:
An Interactive Chinese Multimodal Emotion Dataset With Rich Annotations",
[arXiv:2505.23018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23018)) is Mandarin dyadic dialogue recorded
by 19 professional actors from a drama department: 744 dialogues of about two minutes,
19,250 utterances, 23.6 hours, with audio, video and text. The dialogues follow situational
scripts (inspired by television plots or generated by LLMs and reviewed by hand); the
paper says the actors "are not constrained by the script" and are encouraged to express
authentic emotions on the theme (Section 3.1).

## Labels

Seven labels, mapped to lower case: `neutral`, `angry`, `happy`, `sad`, `disgust`
(disgusted), `fear` (fearful) and `surprise` (surprised).

The dataset has separate labels for audio, text, silent video and video with audio. The
recipe uses the **audio** labels: `emotion_result` in `Audio/json/.../<utterance>.json`.
For these, five annotators who could only listen to the clip each chose one emotion with a
confidence (0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7 or 0.9, stored as 1, 3, 5, 7, 9); the final label is the
emotion with the highest weighted confidence, and experts decided cases with low
confidence or disagreement (paper Section 3.2). The audio labels differ from the counts on
the Hugging Face dataset page, which are those of the video-with-audio labels (for example
6,853 neutral there against 9,378 here).

## Listening study

The annotation above is a perception test by people who only heard the audio (paper
Section 3.2; the paper does not say how annotators were recruited or trained). Per
utterance: 5 annotators, the same five (A to E) for every utterance.

- Fleiss' kappa of the audio labels is 0.79 (paper Section 3.3; text 0.66, silent video 0.73,
  video with audio 0.78).
- The per-annotator answers are in the download (`data` in each json file). Measured here:
  all five agree on 12,700 of 19,250 utterances (66.0%), and an annotator's answer equals the
  final label in 88,642 of 96,250 cases (92.1%).

## Data

Measured on the extracted `Audio` folder (all 19,250 wav files listed by the json files
are present and readable):

- 19,250 utterances, 23.60 hours, 0.31 to 24.90 s long (median 3.56 s). 19 speakers in
  15 two-person groups.
- Audio is 44.1 kHz in 19,130 files (18,612 stereo, 518 mono) and 16 kHz mono in 120 files.
  In a random sample of 1,500 files, 1,440 are stereo: in 1,031 the two channels are identical,
  151 correlate above 0.9 and 258 below 0.9, and none has a silent channel (the lowest
  correlations were not examined). The recipe averages the channels to mono.
- 6,647 utterances (35%) contain `[over/]...[/over]` marks in the transcript: speech that
  overlaps with the other speaker.
- No byte-identical files and no near-duplicates (correlation 0.98 or more) were found.

## Environment variables

Both are optional.

- `EMOTIONTALK` - a directory holding the extracted `Audio` folder, or where to download
  it (default `download/` under the recipe)
- `EMOTIONTALK_OUTPUT` - where the converted audio and manifests are written (default `data/`)

## Download

The Hugging Face dataset `BAAI/Emotiontalk` is gated. **Before the first run, sign in to
Hugging Face, open the dataset page and agree to share your username and email with the
authors** (access is then granted immediately), and make your token available
(`huggingface-cli login` or `$HF_TOKEN`). Without this, `prepare_source` stops with an
error that says so.

`prepare_source` downloads `Audio.tar` (14.8 GB), extracts it (about 15 GB more) and
deletes the archive. Measured: the download took about 7 minutes; `create_dataset`
(converting 19,250 files with 16 parallel ffmpeg jobs) took about 2 minutes;
`collect_stats` took about 34 seconds.

## Splits

The published partition by speaker group is used: groups `G00001` and `G00012` are valid,
`G00003` and `G00015` are test, the other eleven are train (15,413 / 1,908 / 1,929
utterances, as on the dataset page). Speakers in train do not appear in valid or test;
valid and test share two speakers.

`remove_long_short` drops utterances shorter than 0.2 s or longer than 30 s; no utterance
is affected, and test is not filtered.

## Quick start

Run the three commands separately. With several GPUs, `train` starts one
process per GPU by re-running its own command, so any other stage listed in the
same command would run once per GPU as well.

```bash
python run.py --stages create_dataset remove_long_short prepare_labels \
    collect_stats --training_config conf/training.yaml
python run.py --stages train \
    --training_config conf/training.yaml
python run.py --stages infer measure \
    --training_config conf/training.yaml \
    --inference_config conf/inference.yaml \
    --metrics_config conf/metrics.yaml
```
